Two key pillars of populism

According to author Jan-Werner Müller, populism requires two important facets. For one, it must be anti-elitist in that it pits a "majority" or a "true people" against an "other" or an elite that has been failing them politically. Second, it must be anti-pluralist in that it offers itself as the only solution to any political, economic, or social issues. The us-versus-them idea encompasses both pitting the people against politicians or parties who have failed them but also against any other ideologies that could offer solutions to the issues being faced.

Why two pillars and not one?

Without both pillars, the ideology essentially collapses, or certainly does not actually constitute populism. Anti-elitism can be present in a wide range of political ideologies or parties, and is often beneficial in creating structural change. Anti-pluralism alone can constitute various forms of political ideology, namely authoritarianism. Offering only one solution to governance or politics doesn't necessitate a true representation of the people.
